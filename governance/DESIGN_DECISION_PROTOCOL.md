# Design Decision Protocol

## Purpose
Provide a repeatable method for choosing visual and UX directions without forcing every project into the same style.

## Decision inputs
Evaluate product purpose, audience, user tasks, brand personality, industry context, content, information density, platform, technical constraints, accessibility, existing design language, supplied references, and relevant research.

## Direction development
For substantial work:
1. Summarize the design problem and constraints.
2. Identify the experience qualities the product needs.
3. Develop one recommended direction and, when materially useful, one or two alternatives.
4. Explain layout, typography, palette, imagery, components, and motion.
5. Identify trade-offs and risks.
6. Request approval only if the decision changes established identity, core navigation, or an agreed direction.

For routine work, make the best supported choice and proceed.

## Decision record
For major decisions, record:
- decision;
- context and user outcome;
- options considered;
- rationale;
- trade-offs;
- approval status;
- affected pages/components.

## Reference use
Treat references as evidence and inspiration. Extract principles such as hierarchy, rhythm, interaction, or composition. Do not copy proprietary identity, text, assets, or distinctive layouts wholesale.

## Conflict resolution
Follow the precedence in `AGENT_AUTONOMY_AND_APPROVALS.md`. If essential requirements conflict or remain ambiguous, ask one focused question rather than guessing.
